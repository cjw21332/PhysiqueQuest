# PushUpBench dataset audit (Phase 2)

Generated by `ml/scripts/74_audit_pattern_data.py`. Machine-readable output:
`ml/reports/pattern_v1_data_audit.json`; per-video review sheet:
`ml/reports/label_review.csv` (231 rows, one per extracted clip).

Scope: the 231 extracted clips only. No model is evaluated here.

## 1. Technical health (strong)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Clips | 231 |
| Resolution | 1920×1080, **all 231** |
| Frame rate | 30.0 fps, **all 231** |
| Orientation | landscape, **all 231** |
| Duration | min 22 s, median 32 s, p90 42 s, max 117 s |
| Detection rate | median 100%, p10 100%, min 82.2% |
| Clips below 90% detection | 1 (`cbc15c42_0746_shoulder in & out`, 82.2%) |
| Fully-zero joint slots | mean 0.11%, max 11.1% |
| Clips shorter than one window | 0 |

Keypoint tracking is not the problem: MediaPipe finds a pose on essentially every
sampled frame, and missing-landmark handling barely matters (only 5 of 928 test
windows contain any zero slot). The 46.9% is not a tracking failure.

**Diversity is the problem.** Every clip is 1920×1080 landscape 30 fps. There is
no portrait video, no floor-level phone angle, no low-resolution clip, no
zoom/framing variation. A model trained here sees one camera condition only,
which is a direct risk for phone-in-hand portrait use in PhysiqueQuest.

## 2. Preprocessing defect: visibility is destroyed

`FEATURE_SPEC.md` §2 applies the centring/scaling to all four columns:

```
kps_centered = kps - mid_hip          # visibility included
kps_normalized = kps_centered / torso # visibility included
```

Visibility is a 0–1 confidence, so centring it on the mid-hip visibility and
dividing by torso length turns it into an unbounded, sign-flipped number. The
audit measures median key-joint visibility **−1.06** (range −2.61 to −0.07) —
mathematically valid, semantically meaningless.

Consequences:

- The model cannot use keypoint confidence, so it cannot down-weight frames
  where wrists/ankles are occluded.
- `ml/scripts/71_extract_videos.py` reproduces the spec faithfully; the defect is
  in the spec, not the extractor.
- No TypeScript implementation of §2 exists in the repo (`shared/pose-features.ts`
  is referenced by the spec but absent), so fixing the spec breaks no shipped
  consumer — but it *does* change model input semantics, so it must be a
  versioned spec change (v1.1) and v2 models must be trained on it.

## 3. Rep annotations vs the movement signal

Crude check: count peaks in the highest-range joint axis and compare with the
annotated rep count. This is a review flag, not proof of label error.

| Class | Single-set clips | Median abs error | Within ±1 | Median GT reps | Median peaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| core_flexion | 81 | 4 | 43.2% | 12 | 8 |
| hinge | 76 | 4 | 42.1% | 12 | 11 |
| cardio_dynamic | 20 | 7 | 30.0% | 17 | 8.5 |
| squat | 13 | 1 | 53.8% | 20 | 14 |
| core_isometric | 11 | 6 | 9.1% | 10 | 3 |
| lateral_raise | 6 | 1.5 | 50.0% | 10 | 10.5 |
| horizontal_push | 4 | 7 | 25.0% | 10.5 | 1.5 |

Aggregate: 215 single-set clips, median abs error 4, 40% within ±1, r = 0.233.

Two clusters deserve attention:

- **horizontal_push**: annotated ~10.5 reps but the signal shows 1.5. Either the
  dominant-axis heuristic fails for push-ups (expected: elbow/shoulder motion is
  in a plane the heuristic misses), or the clips are mostly setup/rest. `wall
  push` (10 reps → 2 peaks) and `commando` (6 → 0) are the extremes.
- **core_isometric**: annotated ~10.5 reps → 3 peaks. Plank variants are
  partly isometric, so "reps" may mean holds or small pulses; the annotation
  semantics differ from the other classes.

Neither is enough evidence to relabel anything. They are recorded as review
items in `label_review.csv`.

## 4. Label ambiguity (mapping-level, quantified)

PushUpBench names are mapped to 9 patterns by regex table. Some mapped names are
movements that do not match the pattern they are assigned to:

| Pattern | Clips | Of which arguably misfit | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| core_isometric | 12 | **4** | `plank + jumping jack`, `PLANK + JUMPING JACK`, `plank jump + 3 punches` (dynamic), `bridge` (hip extension) |
| squat | 13 | **3** | `step jacks` (cardio), `reverse lunges`, `reverse lunges challenge` (lunge/hip-dominant) |
| lateral_raise | 6 | **3** | `wall twist` (rotation), `warrior 2 + up` ×2 (yoga pose) |
| cardio_dynamic | 26 | 0–2 | mostly jumping jacks / high knees; `squat + side jumpy` variants sit in `squat` |
| hinge | 82 | 0–2 | broad hip-hinge family, internally consistent |
| core_flexion | 83 | 0–2 | broad crunch family, internally consistent |
| horizontal_push | 5 | 0 | all push-up variants |

This is **not** an extraction error and **not** something to "fix" by relabeling
without the dataset authors; the mapping is intentionally coarse. But it is a
real cause of the confusion matrix:

- `core_isometric → cardio_dynamic` was 71.6% of that class. Three of the twelve
  `core_isometric` clips contain jumping jacks. The model is partly right and the
  label is partly wrong.
- `squat ↔ cardio_dynamic` (56 test errors) is consistent with `step jacks` /
  `squat + side jumpy` being labelled squat.
- `lateral_raise` recall 0.0 is consistent with a four-subject class whose test
  subject's clips (`sun rises`, `wall twist`) are not classic lateral raises.

Implication for v2: evaluate a "clean-label" variant that drops the 10 misfit
clips **from train and validation only**, keeping the test set untouched and
reporting both numbers. That isolates label noise from model capacity without
tuning on test.

## 5. Identity coverage and splits

| Class | Videos | Subjects | Subject count is the limiting factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| core_flexion | 83 | 18 | adequate |
| hinge | 82 | 17 | adequate |
| cardio_dynamic | 26 | 10 | thin |
| squat | 13 | 6 | thin |
| core_isometric | 12 | **6** | thin, and ambiguous labels |
| horizontal_push | 5 | **5** | marginal |
| lateral_raise | 6 | **4** | insufficient |
| curl | 3 | 1 | excluded |
| triceps_extension | 1 | 1 | excluded |
| horizontal_pull | 0 | 0 | absent |

A three-way subject-disjoint split needs ≥3 subjects per class to place one in
each split, and realistically ≥8–10 to make val/test meaningful. Under that
standard only `core_flexion` and `hinge` qualify; `cardio_dynamic`, `squat`,
`core_isometric`, `horizontal_push` and `lateral_raise` are below it, which is
exactly where the per-class F1 collapses (0.35, 0.63, 0.05, 0.35, 0.00).

Decision: keep `curl`, `triceps_extension` and `horizontal_pull` excluded. There
is no evidence to add them, and the user instruction is not to inflate class
count.

## 6. Duplicates and ID aliases

- **Exact duplicate footage across splits.** `cbc15c42_0616_3 point knee tap
  (L)_[9]` and `cbc15c42a1594949711ac9a7c8c465e1_0616_3 point knee tap (L)_[9]`
  are byte-identical keypoint sequences. The short-hash clip is in **val**, the
  full-hash clip is in **train**. Same person, same video, both sides of the
  split.
- 2 alias collisions: `199aec58` / `199aec58ef05…`, `cbc15c42` / `cbc15c42a159…`.
  Real subject count is 28, not 30.
- 28 subject/exercise pairs repeat within a subject (e.g. `87fe2aa4|squat` ×3,
  `fc3d98d5` six pairs ×2). These are separate recordings, not duplicates — fine
  for data volume, but they inflate window counts for one identity.

Fix required before any v2 run: canonicalise subject IDs to the 8-char prefix,
deduplicate identical sequences, and rebuild splits.

## 7. Verdict on dataset sufficiency

- Labels: usable but noisy at the class boundary (~10 clips of 231 clearly
  ambiguous; concentrated in the three weakest classes).
- Keypoints: high quality; tracking is not a bottleneck.
- Rep annotations: reliable for squat / lateral_raise / hinge, ambiguous for
  core_isometric and (by signal) horizontal_push.
- Diversity: **single camera condition** (1080p landscape 30 fps) — the biggest
  structural limitation after subject count.
- Coverage: 2 of 7 classes have adequate identity coverage; 3 are at or below the
  minimum for a subject-disjoint split.

Conclusion: this dataset can support a baseline and controlled architecture
experiments, but **cannot** produce a G4-level (0.85) model, no matter the
architecture, because 3 classes have 4–6 identities. More subjects are required,
which makes the PhysiqueQuest recording pipeline (Phase 3) the critical path.
